The Museo Nacional de Bellas Artes is arguably the finest art gallery in
the Caribbean, spread across two buildings just blocks apart in Havana.
The Palacio de Bellas Artes holds the most comprehensive collection of
Cuban art anywhere — more than 45,000 works spanning colonial times to
today, including pieces by Wifredo Lam — while the Palacio del Centro
Asturiano houses the museum's Universal Art collection inside a grand
eclectic palace overlooking Parque Central. We move through both at an
unhurried pace, with a specialist guide framing the pieces that define
Cuban art history.

The day closes with something far rarer: a private visit to the studio
of *Mateo Aguilar (mock-up name)*, envisioned as an established,
internationally exhibited Cuban surrealist artist working in the
tradition of the island's most celebrated living painters and
sculptors. Seeing an artist's actual working space, in conversation with
them directly, is an entirely different experience from seeing their
work on a gallery wall.
