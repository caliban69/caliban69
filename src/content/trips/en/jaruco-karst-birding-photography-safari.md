Escaleras de Jaruco is a protected karstic region a short drive from Havana
that mirrors the biodiversity of Viñales while staying off the main tourist
trail. Vertical mogotes and lush tropical flora provide a high-contrast
backdrop for photographing Cuba's most emblematic birds in an intimate,
tranquil setting.

Unlike the flat wetlands of Zapata, Jaruco's varying low elevations put
photographers at eye level with canopy-dwelling species. The day is built
around the golden hours of early morning and late afternoon, using the
dramatic shadows of the karst cliffs to add depth and mood to every frame.

**Morning — the endemic trio.** We arrive at dawn to catch the ethereal
Cuban Solitaire performing its flute-like song from limestone perches, then
turn to the Cuban Tody and Cuban Trogon as the light softens — their red,
white, and blue plumage set against Jaruco's rich greens and limestone
greys.

**Midday — the hidden owls.** During the brighter hours we move into shaded
forest galleries to track the Cuban Pygmy-Owl and the Bare-legged Owl,
focused on their textures and intense gazes hidden within the foliage.

**Afternoon — forest life and the golden exit.** We close the day with the
Cuban Green Woodpecker and shorebird activity, working the warm, low-angle
light hitting the mangrove before the return to Havana.

**Meet your photography mentor: Freddy.** With over 30 years in the field
and a university background in conservation, Freddy knows the specific
limestone "theaters" where Jaruco's endemics appear — and, as a
photographer himself, he guides each session toward the interaction between
subject, light, and the karst landscape, not just a species checklist.
